## Decisive main-text/appendix reconciliation

There is **no missing-Macaulay-bracket discrepancy**. Appendix A12, printed p. 418, visibly brackets the plastic volumetric increment. Extraction dropped the angled glyphs. Main Eqs. 5 and 9, plus the explicit explanation on p. 405, establish its positive-part meaning. Plastic dilation suppresses this rotational contribution; it does not reverse it and must not be replaced by an absolute value.

Main Eq. 9 has an absolute value around signed triaxial plastic shear increment. Appendix A6 defines the equivalent deviatoric increment by the square-root-of-2/3 tensor norm; interpreted as its nonnegative magnitude, this gives the same absolute value upon triaxial specialization. A12 therefore requires no extra signed-shear branch. The reviewer checked compression, extension, zero increment and negative plastic volume algebraically. This is the original law, not a repaired variant.

Appendix A4 uses square-root-of-two tensor shear entries, equivalently engineering shear divided by square root of two. A1/A7 use matching symmetric stress/fabric representation. ## Calibration, preparation and experimental limits

Equations 11–14, pp. 409–411, derive an approximate initial K0 inclination and beta recipe by neglecting elastic strains relative to plastic strains during one-dimensional consolidation. It is suitable for normally consolidated or lightly overconsolidated histories. Heavy overconsolidation can cause plastic rotation during unloading, so the recipe is not a universal reconstruction from current K0/OCR. Current stress, specific volume, yield size and full fabric must be supplied consistently; a scalar K0 estimate alone cannot initialize arbitrary orientation.

With eta denoting the normally consolidated K0 stress ratio, Eq. 13 gives alpha=(eta²+3eta−M²)/3; Eq. 14 gives beta=3(4M²−4eta²−3eta)/[8(eta²−M²+2eta)]. Jaky's estimate is optional preparation evidence, not a governing constitutive equation or exact inference. Lambda is the compression slope for constant stress ratio with unchanged anisotropy, not every observed apparent slope during rotating fabric.

The original's suggested mu range is **10/lambda to 15/lambda**, pp. 412 and 416, supported as a fallback estimate. It differs from the earlier presentation's heuristic and should supersede that slide for this journal identity. Calibration through simulation of anisotropy-changing tests is preferred. The Otaniemi comparison uses lambda 0.44, kappa 0.04, M 1.1, Poisson ratio 0.2, initial inclination 0.42, beta 0.67, and investigates mu 10, 20 and 50. Table 4 supplies sample-specific volume/size pairs, not a universal initial state.

Tables 1–2 and Figs. 3–6 include the data used to choose yield/rotation ingredients; Fig. 9 comparisons and parameter studies must not be relabelled independent held-out validation. Source results improve yield-stress and strain-direction predictions but substantially underpredict some post-yield volume changes, attributed to destructuration. The latter is excluded rather than silently fixed by adding S-CLAY1S.
