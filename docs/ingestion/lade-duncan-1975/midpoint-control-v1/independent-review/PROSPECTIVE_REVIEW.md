Compared with failed v3, v4 changes the elastic modulus sampling to the least eigenvalue of the arithmetic tensor mean, E=Kur*pa*lambda_min((S_n+S)/(2pa))^n. It does not replace that eigenvalue by an average of endpoint eigenvalues. Endpoint plastic flow, the fixed-kappa2 flow derivative, local Eq19 without da, current least-pressure plastic coefficient, signed q/decoded H history, initialization, zero-load identity, pre-failure domain, parameter values and mechanical zero-pore fixed-frame embedding remain explicit inherited choices. Midpoint sampling is a declared numerical realization of the differential law, not an algorithm prescribed by the source. The residual division by positive previous dimensionless least pressure preserves its zero set.

For unloading u>v>0 and a>0,n>0, the scalar endpoint step is h=(u-v)/(a*((u+v)/2)^n). Convexity of x^(-n) gives h <= integral_v^u x^(-n)dx/a. For 0<n<1, summing over any finite sequence of positive endpoints gives total supplied unloading strictly less than u0^(1-n)/((1-n)a). Thus an exact positive-endpoint midpoint sequence cannot complete or cross finite extinction. Accepting two midpoint halfsteps preserves this telescoping inequality. The positive scalar endpoint branch is unique: its step length decreases continuously from 2^n*u^(1-n)/a at v=0 to zero at v=u.

For S=pa*u*B with fixed SPD B, nu_i=0, and proportional loading, f is constant and below ft for the tested B eigenvalues(1.1,1,.9). Plastic work is zero and a=Kur*lambda_min(B)^n. The proof does not cover arbitrary rotating/nonproportional three-dimensional paths, active plasticity, mixed-sign elastic paths or finite-precision residual defects. Smooth constant-f elasticity is second order; endpoint plastic sampling retains general first-order accuracy.
