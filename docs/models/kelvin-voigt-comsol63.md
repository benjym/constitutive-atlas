# Kelvin–Voigt — COMSOL 6.3 ordinary deviatoric model

The source-linked formulation places an isotropic spring and dashpot in parallel in the deviatoric response, with purely elastic bulk. It uses physical seconds, three positive parameters K (Pa), G (Pa), tau (s), and six persistent infinitesimal strain components. The viscosity is eta=G tau in Pa s. Source: [COMSOL 6.3 Linear Viscoelasticity, Eq 3-36](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_theory.06.029.html#3441080), and [ordinary Kelvin–Voigt settings](https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.sme/sme_ug_solid.07.016.html#4898977).

The law is epsdot=D and sigma=K tr(eps) I+2G dev(eps)+2G tau dev(D). Stress is dry total Cauchy stress. Strain and stress compression are positive; Mandel order is xx,yy,zz,sqrt(2)xy,sqrt(2)yz,sqrt(2)xz. Small strain and a fixed frame with zero spin are explicit restrictions. There is no pore pressure, volumetric viscosity, auxiliary branch memory, thermal dependence or finite rotation adapter.

Every initial strain and incoming rate is supplied explicitly. Initial total stress depends on both. A strain-rate jump leaves strain continuous and immediately changes viscous stress. At zero strain rate the viscous term disappears; subsequent strain hold maintains elastic stress. Under held deviatoric stress, strain creeps exponentially with retardation time tau. An ideal strain impulse is outside finite-rate controls.

The main laboratory offers constant rate, strain hold and stress hold, all six components, complete state/stress tables, component plots, reset and verified export/import/replay. The illustrative creep preparation uses K=20MPa,G=12MPa,tau=2s, zero initial strain and incoming deviatoric rates [.001,-.0005,-.0005,.0004,-.0002,.0003]/s. It then holds the resulting initial stress for eight seconds. These values are synthetic examples, without calibration or experimental validation.
