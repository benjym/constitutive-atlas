# Normalized determinant: numerical contract v1

Prospective generic operation `normalized_determinant(A)` means det(A)/(trace(A))³ on the exact real values represented by six finite binary64 components of a symmetric tensor in orthonormal Mandel order xx,yy,zz,sqrt2xy,sqrt2yz,sqrt2xz. The operation returns a dimensionless scalar for any consistent tensor unit. This mathematical identity also equals det(A/trace(A)) over exact reals; the implementation must not first round the six normalized components. This is an arithmetic capability, not a new constitutive equation or source-domain restriction.

The trace is the exact sum of the original three diagonal components; it need not itself fit binary64. An exactly zero trace rejects, including when determinant is also zero. All finite signs/indefinite tensors remain arithmetic inputs; constitutive support is a separate declarative gate. Do not impose an SPD guard, stress floor or magnitude threshold inside the operation. Intermediate determinant or trace-cube overflow/underflow must not reject a representable final ratio. Rounding is nearest-even binary64 at the final ratio, with canonical +0 for rounded underflow; final overflow rejects.

Write T=trace(A), P=det(A). For each of38finite stored seed directions E, the mathematical directional derivative is

`(Ddet(A)[E]*T - 3*P*trace(E))/T^4`.

Every primal and directional derivative is independently rounded once at its exact value. Differentiate the mathematical ratio at the stored primal, not the discontinuous binary64 rounding map, a normalized tensor, or separately rounded P/T/cofactors. All six primals and228seed entries must be finite even when coefficients vanish. If any final primal/derivative overflows or any input/seed is nonfinite, reject and leave the entire caller output unchanged. Primal evaluation may succeed while Jet evaluation rejects a derivative overflow. A zero trace always rejects. Allocation failure may propagate without assigning caller output.

Implement exact dyadic/radical numerators and a signed exact dyadic denominator, reusing the unchanged determinant core's audited integer operations. Convert the denominator to positive by negating both numerator coefficients as needed. To compare a quotient with a dyadic rounding boundary y, compare the exact radical numerator with y*denominator. Establish signs before reducing radical comparisons to integer squares. A floating approximation may propose a cell only; exact midpoint comparisons certify it. Otherwise search the complete ordered finite binary64 range. No rational division or rounded sqrt2 coefficient is needed in C++.

The primal numerator has degree3; its denominator is T³. The derivative numerator has degree4 counting the seed, its denominator T⁴. For finite binary64 primals/seeds, conservative exponent spans and product/square sizes must be checked in review; dynamic unsigned32bit limbs retain exact cancellation, and no hardware integer truncation is allowed.
